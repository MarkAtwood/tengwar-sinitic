# Tengwar Mode for Jin (Taiyuan)

## Romanization Disclaimer

> **WARNING: No standardized romanization exists for Jin Chinese.**
>
> This mode uses IPA as the primary reference, with a project-defined romanization as a convenience layer. The romanization follows Pinyin conventions where phonemes overlap with Mandarin; Jin-specific sounds use systematic extensions.
>
> Readers familiar with Jin phonology should refer to the IPA transcriptions as authoritative.

---

## Why this mode exists

Jin is the eighth Sinitic language to receive a Tengwar mode and the first to use the Sinitic IPA intermediate layer directly. Spoken by approximately 45 million people in Shanxi Province and neighboring areas, Jin was historically grouped with Mandarin but has distinct phonological features that warrant separate treatment.

The key distinguishing features:
- **Preserved entering tone** — final glottal stop /ʔ/ from Middle Chinese *-p, *-t, *-k
- **Voiced fricatives** — /v, z, ɣ, ʐ/, absent in Mandarin
- **Six tones** including a checked tone category

This mode uses Taiyuan (太原) dialect as the reference variety, the provincial capital and prestige dialect of Jin.

This is a phonemic tehtar mode.

---

## Compatibility Group

Jin belongs to **Group B** (voicing semantics), like Min and Wu:
- Grade 1 = voiceless
- Grade 2 = voiced
- Aspiration marked with diacritic where contrastive

This differs from Mandarin/Cantonese/Hakka/Gan/Xiang (Group A), despite Jin's geographic proximity to Mandarin-speaking areas. The presence of voiced fricatives places Jin phonologically closer to the southern branches.

### Aspiration marking

In Group B modes, aspiration is marked with a diacritic (breath mark above) rather than Grade 2. This preserves Grade 2 for the voicing contrast.

### Voiced fricatives

Jin's voiced fricatives /v, z, ɣ, ʐ/ are the key Group B feature. These map to:
- /v/ — ampa or modified vala
- /z/ — esse (standard Tengwar for /z/)
- /ɣ/ — harma with voicing mark
- /ʐ/ — hwesta with voicing mark (same position as voiceless /ʂ/)

## Final consonants

### Nasal coda

| IPA | Rom. | Tengwa |
|-----|------|--------|
| ŋ | ng | noldo |

Note: Taiyuan has merged most nasal codas to /ŋ/ or nasalized vowels. Historical *-n and *-m distinctions are largely lost.

### Glottal stop (entering tone)

| IPA | Rom. | Tengwa |
|-----|------|--------|
| ʔ | q | telco (or ungwe) |

The preserved glottal stop is Jin's most distinctive feature versus Mandarin. Syllables ending in /ʔ/ carry the entering tone (checked tone category).

The glottal stop is written as a final tengwa. This makes checked syllables visually distinct from open syllables.

## Tones

Taiyuan has five citation tones plus tone sandhi.

### Tone inventory

| Tone | Category | Chao | Description | Mark |
|------|----------|------|-------------|------|
| T1 | Yin Ping | ˨˩˧ (213) | dipping | chevron |
| T2 | Yang Ping | ˧ (33) | mid level | flat bar + dot |
| T3 | Shang | ˦˩˨ (412) | high-falling-dipping | falling + chevron |
| T4 | Qu | ˥˧ (53) | high-falling | falling stroke |
| T5 | Ru (checked) | ˥ʔ (5ʔ) | high, glottal | flat bar |

### Tone mark rationale

- **T1 (213)**: Dipping contour — chevron mark
- **T2 (33)**: Mid level — flat bar with mid-register dot
- **T3 (412)**: Complex falling-dipping — falling stroke (primary) + chevron modifier
- **T4 (53)**: Falling from high — falling stroke
- **T5 (5ʔ)**: High checked — flat bar; glottal stop coda implies checked

## Syllable structure

```
(Initial) (Medial) Nucleus (Coda) Tone
```

- Initial: any consonant or zero
- Medial: /j, w, ɥ/ glides
- Nucleus: vowel or diphthong
- Coda: /ŋ/ or /ʔ/ (no other codas)
- Tone: one of five

Maximum: CGVC (e.g., /kuaŋ˥˧/)

## Comparison with Mandarin

| Feature | Mandarin | Jin | Impact |
|---------|----------|-----|--------|
| Group | A (aspiration) | B (voicing) | Different Grade 2 semantics |
| Voiced fricatives | none | v, z, ɣ, ʐ | +4 consonants |
| Entering tone | lost | preserved (ʔ) | +1 coda, +1 tone category |
| Retroflex | yes | yes | Same Column III |
| Palatals | yes | yes | Same palatal mark |

## Font requirements

Jin requires the same glyph inventory as other Group B modes (Min, Wu):
- Aspiration diacritic (for distinguishing pʰ from p in Grade 1)
- Voicing marker (for distinguishing voiced fricatives)
- Standard tone marks and register modifiers

All glyphs available in Alcarin Extended.

## Open questions

1. **Subdialect variation**: Significant variation exists across Jin-speaking areas. Taiyuan is the reference; other varieties may differ in tone values and vowel inventory.

2. **Voiced fricative representation**: The optimal Tengwar assignment for /v, z, ɣ, ʐ/ in Group B needs validation against Min/Wu conventions.

3. **T3 complexity**: The 412 contour is unusually complex. The falling+chevron combination may need refinement.
